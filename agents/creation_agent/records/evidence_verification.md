## Claim mapping and qualifications

- Primary v2 evidence #1-5 map to S1, S2, S4, S5 and S6; #7 maps to S7. Contrast v2 #1, #2, #4-7 map to S1-S6. S3 repairs a difference in coverage, not a contradiction in historical evidence.
- Keep the chronology stable across the two contexts. ReAct and Toolformer represent different approaches; the list is a synthesis of relevant developments, not a proved linear causal chain.
- The strongest documented non-technical condition here is commercial distribution through APIs and the historical reduction of specified API prices (S6; supplementary API announcement above). Its likely effect on experimentation costs is an explicitly labelled inference. No evidence quantifies its causal contribution relative to research, demand or other conditions.
- The retailer's catalog, APIs and staffing constraints and the hospital's systems, risk posture and approval requirements are supplied hypothetical case assumptions. They have not been verified against real organizations.
- Buying safeguards follow from both case constraints and the fallibility of models connected to tools. The source evidence supports a bounded interpretation, not a claim that human approval eliminates risk.
- Replace “current tooling reduces the retailer's engineering burden” with “reusable interfaces may reduce some custom work; actual integration effort remains unmeasured.” Avoid unsupported forecasts of conversion, ROI, readiness or full autonomy.

## Final bounded synthesis

**ET generally:** the reviewed literature documents attention-based models, in-context task capability, alignment with user intent, and research patterns coupling models to external actions. Commercial tool interfaces and historically lower access costs offered an implementation environment for experimentation. This is a multi-part evolution, with uncertain causal weights, rather than a single invention story.

**Shopping application:** use language interpretation and tool coordination for product discovery and comparison, while catalog, price, inventory and cart state come from retailer-controlled systems. S7 is historical evidence that grounded shopping is difficult; it supplies no contemporary success forecast.

**Retailer:** its assumed commerce APIs make a bounded pilot worth considering within the stated 12 months, conditional on integration and evaluation evidence. Keep purchase completion under explicit human authorization. Limited engineering capacity strengthens the case for a small scope, not a conclusion that the organization is ready.

**Hospital contrast:** consider read-mostly retrieval from approved sources and reviewable procurement drafts over 18-24 months. Restrict state-changing tools and retain staff purchasing authority. This is a context-sensitive inference; the general technology evidence remains the same.

**Judgment:** suitable for team discussion with source review and these qualifications. The two saved contexts demonstrate useful differentiation but do not establish repeatability, deployment safety, market demand, or an empirical benefit. Hand off readiness, value measurement, security, privacy and legal questions to the relevant specialists.
